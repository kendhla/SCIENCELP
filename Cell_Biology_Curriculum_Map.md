# Cell Biology Curriculum Mapping

## Unit 1: Cell Biology

---

### Topic 1: The Cell

#### Content Standard
1. The cell theory is significant in explaining the structure and function of all living organisms
2. Cells can be classified according to distinguishing structures and functions

#### Learning Competencies
Students will be able to:

| # | Learning Competency | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Use information from secondary sources to prepare a historical analysis to explain the significant contributions of scientists (Robert Hooke, Antoine van Leeuwenhoek, Matthias Schleiden, Rudolf Virchow) to the development of cell theory | Understand/Analyze |
| 2 | Use information from secondary sources to explain the differences and advantages between light microscope and electron microscopes in the study of cells | Understand/Compare |
| 3 | Describe the four main parts of the modern cell theory | Remember/Understand |
| 4 | Explain by drawing scaled diagrams the differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells and their significance in diversity and classification of life forms | Understand/Apply |

#### Performance Standards
Students demonstrate mastery when they:
- Successfully identify key scientists and their contributions to cell theory
- Accurately compare microscopy techniques
- Can articulate all four parts of modern cell theory
- Create accurate, scaled diagrams with clear labels and explanations

#### Assessments
- **Formative**: Historical analysis worksheet on scientist contributions
- **Formative**: Comparison chart (light vs. electron microscopes)
- **Formative**: Concept quiz on cell theory
- **Summative**: Scaled diagram project with written explanation
- **Summative**: Cell classification assessment

#### Strategies/Activities
- Research project on history of cell theory
- Virtual microscopy lab
- Diagram creation and labeling exercises
- Cooperative learning: student presentations on key scientists
- Interactive digital simulations comparing cell types

### Topic 2: The Cell Cycle

#### Content Standard
The cell cycle consists of a series of phases, stages, and events

#### Learning Competencies
Students will be able to:

| # | Learning Competency | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Explain the phases of the cell cycle (interphase, M-phase, and cytokinesis) and their control points | Understand/Explain |
| 6 | Differentiate the stages and main features of cell division in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells | Understand/Compare |
| 7 | Explain the processes of crossing over and recombination in meiosis that result in genetic variation among meiotic offspring | Understand/Explain |
| 8 | Explain the significance of mitosis in growth, repair, regeneration of damaged cells, and meiosis for reproduction | Understand/Analyze |

#### Performance Standards
Students demonstrate mastery when they:
- Identify and describe all phases of the cell cycle with control points
- Distinguish between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell division
- Explain mechanisms of genetic variation through crossing over
- Connect cell division processes to biological significance

#### Assessments
- **Formative**: Cell cycle diagram with labeled phases
- **Formative**: Comparison chart (mitosis vs. meiosis)
- **Formative**: Quiz on genetic recombination
- **Summative**: Cell cycle animation project
- **Summative**: Written explanation of significance of cell division types

#### Strategies/Activities
- Interactive cell cycle simulation software
- Microscope observation of prepared slides
- Creating animated models or videos of cell division
- Case studies linking cell division to growth and repair
- Cooperative learning stations for different phases

### Topic 3: Transport Mechanisms

#### Content Standard
Cells have mechanisms that regulate the transport of substances in and out of the cell

#### Learning Competencies
Students will be able to:

| # | Learning Competency | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Describe the transport mechanisms in cells, including passive transport (diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion) and active transport (pumps, endocytosis, exocytosis) | Understand/Describe |
| 10 | Explain the role of the cell membrane in maintaining cellular function and homeostasis | Understand/Explain |
| 11 | Use information from secondary sources to explain how cellular abnormalities contribute to diseases (cancer, diabetes, genetic disorders) | Understand/Analyze |

#### Performance Standards
Students demonstrate mastery when they:
- Accurately describe all transport mechanisms with examples
- Explain the relationship between membrane structure and function
- Connect cellular abnormalities to disease processes

#### Assessments
- **Formative**: Transport mechanism matching activity
- **Formative**: Diagram of cell membrane with transport types labeled
- **Suggested Performance Task**: Scientific investigation of diffusion using water, iodine, and starch solution
- **Summative**: Written explanation connecting transport to homeostasis
- **Summative**: Research report on cellular abnormalities and disease

#### Strategies/Activities
- **Hands-on Lab**: Diffusion investigation using semipermeable membrane
  - Materials: water, iodine solution, starch solution
  - Observe molecular movement across membrane
  - Record observations and draw conclusions
- Animated simulations of transport mechanisms
- Case study analysis of diseases related to transport dysfunction
- Concept mapping for transport types
- Think-pair-share on homeostasis

## Unit 2: Cell Metabolism

---

### Topic 4: Cell Requirements

#### Content Standard
All cells require energy, nutrients, and removal of wastes

#### Learning Competencies
Students will be able to:

| # | Learning Competency | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | Identify that photosynthesis takes place within the cells of autotrophs with the aid of chloroplasts | Remember/Understand |
| 13 | Describe the role of chlorophyll and other pigments in capturing light energy in plants required for photosynthesis reaction | Understand/Describe |

#### Performance Standards
Students demonstrate mastery when they:
- Locate photosynthesis organelles and identify autotrophs
- Explain the function of pigments in light capture

#### Assessments
- **Formative**: Diagram of chloroplast with labeled structures
- **Formative**: Quiz on pigment function
- **Summative**: Explanation of light absorption by pigments

#### Strategies/Activities
- Microscope observation of chloroplasts
- Chromatography experiment separating plant pigments
- Diagram labeling of chloroplast structures
- Digital simulations of light absorption
- Interactive animations of photosystem function

---

### Topic 5: Metabolism - Photosynthesis

#### Content Standard
Photosynthesis is the chemical reaction that transforms light energy into chemical energy

#### Learning Competencies
Students will be able to:

| # | Learning Competency | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | Explain the balanced chemical equation for photosynthesis and synthesis of glucose from CO₂, water, and light | Understand/Explain |
| 15 | Use a diagram to explain key processes of photosynthesis, including light-dependent reactions (ATP/NADPH generation) and light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle/glucose synthesis) | Understand/Apply |
| 16 | Use information from secondary sources to evaluate how photosynthesis can help reduce carbon dioxide levels in the environment | Analyze/Evaluate |

#### Performance Standards
Students demonstrate mastery when they:
- Write and explain the balanced photosynthesis equation
- Create detailed diagrams of light-dependent and light-independent reactions
- Connect photosynthesis to environmental sustainability

#### Assessments
- **Formative**: Equation balancing worksheet
- **Formative**: Diagram interpretation quiz
- **Suggested Performance Task**: Scientific investigation of aquatic plant oxygen production
  - Materials: aquatic plant (Hydrilla, Elodea, or Water Hyacinth), beaker, water, light source
  - Vary light conditions and measure bubble formation rate
  - Create data table, graph, and explanation report
- **Summative**: Annotated photosynthesis pathway diagram
- **Summative**: Environmental research paper on CO₂ reduction

#### Strategies/Activities
- **Hands-on Lab**: Aquatic plant oxygen production investigation
  - Observe oxygen bubbles under different light intensities
  - Record and graph bubble formation rates
  - Write lab report with data analysis
- Equation balancing practice problems
- Digital interactive photosynthesis pathway
- Video analysis of light and dark reactions
- Group research on renewable energy and photosynthesis
- Real-world connection: algae biofuel discussion

### Topic 6: Metabolism - Respiration

#### Content Standard
Cell respiration is the chemical reaction that converts chemical energy into usable forms of energy

#### Learning Competencies
Students will be able to:

| # | Learning Competency | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | Use a diagram to explain steps in aerobic respiration (glycolysis, acetyl-CoA, citric acid cycle, electron transport system, chemiosmosis), including ATP importance and enzyme role | Understand/Apply |
| 18 | Describe how mitochondrial structure relates to function in cellular respiration | Understand/Explain |
| 19 | Differentiate between aerobic and anaerobic respiration, including steps, ATP production, and byproducts | Understand/Compare |
| 20 | Use information from secondary sources to explain how cellular respiration provides energy for everyday activities (running, thinking, digesting food) | Understand/Analyze |

#### Performance Standards
Students demonstrate mastery when they:
- Label and explain all stages of aerobic respiration
- Connect mitochondrial structure to respiratory function
- Compare aerobic and anaerobic processes
- Apply respiration concepts to real-life activities

#### Assessments
- **Formative**: Respiration pathway labeling activity
- **Formative**: Structure-function matching (mitochondria)
- **Formative**: Aerobic vs. anaerobic comparison chart
- **Summative**: Detailed annotated respiration diagram
- **Summative**: Real-world application essay on cellular energy

#### Strategies/Activities
- Detailed diagram creation and annotation
- Mitochondrial structure model building
- Video analysis of cellular respiration steps
- Equation practice: glycolysis through chemiosmosis
- Case studies: respiration in different organisms
- Personal connection: tracking energy use in daily activities
- Interactive digital simulations of respiration
- Research on enzyme function in respiration
- Comparison of plant and animal respiration

## Performance Tasks Summary

### Task 1: Diffusion Investigation
**Standard**: Transport Mechanisms (Topic 3)
**Materials**: Water, iodine solution, starch solution, semipermeable membrane
**Duration**: 1-2 class periods
**Outcome**: Students model passive transport and understand molecular movement

### Task 2: Photosynthesis Investigation
**Standard**: Metabolism - Photosynthesis (Topic 5)
**Materials**: Aquatic plant, beaker, water, light source
**Duration**: 2-3 class periods
**Deliverables**: 
- Data table of bubble formation at different light intensities
- Graph showing relationship between light and photosynthesis rate
- Written report explaining findings and relationship to photosynthesis

## Cross-Cutting Themes

- **Energy Transfer**: Connection between photosynthesis and respiration
- **Structure and Function**: Cell structures related to specific functions
- **Cellular Homeostasis**: Transport mechanisms and metabolic regulation
- **Genetic Variation**: Meiosis and crossing over
- **Scientific Investigation**: Evidence-based learning through labs and observations
- **Real-World Application**: Connecting cellular processes to human health and environmental issues
